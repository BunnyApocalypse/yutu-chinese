Bring your Skritter history with you.

Skritter has no export button, but you can ask its support for a copy of your data. 玉兔中文 reads that file on your own device and carries over your words, their schedules and every review, so cards are due when Skritter said.

What you keep

Your words and their schedules
Each card keeps its due date and how well you know it, so what was due in Skritter is due here. Skritter's file has no pinyin or meanings, so each word is filled in from the app's dictionary.
Your whole review history
Every graded answer comes over as a review, so the app starts from your real history and not from zero. Your reviews count for all four cards of a word: meaning, pinyin, tone and writing.
Your own lists and meanings
Lists you made in Skritter become decks, with a deck for each section. Meanings you wrote yourself are kept, and kinds of card you banned stay suspended.
Traditional and simplified
Each card keeps the script you studied it in.

What doesn't come over

Skritter's own lists, such as HSK levels and textbooks, aren't named in the file. Where nearly all of a list's words belong to one HSK or TOCFL level, the app suggests that level's name. You can name the rest yourself or leave them unnamed.

Mnemonics, Skritter's sentences and its settings aren't brought over. Words the app's dictionary doesn't have, such as zhuyin symbols or phrases, are listed after the import so you can add them by hand.

How to do it

  1. Ask Skritter for your data Open Skritter's contact page, where its privacy policy says to send requests about your data. In the app, Import from Skritter has a ready letter under "How to get your data from Skritter". Say where you live (the EU, EEA or UK, California, or somewhere else), copy the letter, and fill in what is in [brackets]. The letters are in English and are a sample, not legal advice.
  2. Wait for the .zip file Skritter's support answers by email with a .zip file. One learner received theirs in ten days. Where a law applies, it sets a time limit: about a month under the GDPR and 45 days under California's law.
  3. Open the import In the app, go to the Decks tab, choose "+ New / import deck", then "Import from Skritter". Choose the .zip file as it came, without unzipping it.
  4. Check what it found The app tells you how many words, cards and reviews the file holds. Skritter names a word only by its simplified characters, and those can be more than one word, so the app lists them for you to check. Each is ticked; untick any you didn't study. Then name your lists, or leave them unnamed.
  5. Import A large history, well over a hundred thousand reviews, took about ten seconds on a laptop; a tablet takes longer. When it's done, the app says how many words and schedules came over and lists any it left out.

Good to know

The file stays on your device
The import runs in your browser. Your file isn't uploaded anywhere, and the app never asks for your Skritter password.
You can import again
Cards already in the app aren't changed, and a second import adds no duplicates. That lets you import again later, for example after naming more lists.
The schedule carries on
Skritter and 玉兔中文 both schedule with FSRS, so your cards carry on from where Skritter left them.
