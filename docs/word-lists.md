# Ready-made word lists and radicals

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md on 2026-10-15; see `docs/README.md` for where each former section is now.

## Ready-made word lists (HSK and TOCFL)

Asked for by the owner on 2026-10-13: HSK 3.0 first, 2.0 supported too, and TOCFL from the official files.

- **Where:** the "Ready-made decks" section of the Decks tab (see "Decks"). A list is chosen by tapping its row (HSK 3.0, HSK 2.0 marked "older", or TOCFL; rows like those of My decks, each with a line saying what it is, in place of a drop-down list since 2026-10-13, at the owner's request; the chosen one has a heavy edge and HSK 3.0 is chosen to begin with), its levels appear one per row with their counts and an Add button each ("Level 1: 512 words, 480 new [Add]"; checkboxes and one button until 2026-10-14, changed at the owner's request: levels are added one at a time), which adds that level's new words at the end of the queue and puts the words already there into its deck. The radicals, whose levels are one deck, keep checkboxes and one Add button (owner, same day); the hint under the levels says which applies. The chosen list's description is shown whole; the others are cut at two lines. A paragraph above explains what the HSK is and which list to pick, with a link to Wikipedia, for someone new to it.
- **The files** are `data/wordlists/hsk/hsk-3.0.tsv` (11,054 words) and `hsk-2.0.tsv` (5,008), built by `scripts/build-word-lists.js` from two MIT-licensed projects at fixed versions; the folder's README has the sources and the license notice. Columns: level, simplified, traditional, pinyin, meaning. They are separate files (`?url` imports in `src/wordsPanel.js`), fetched when the section is opened and saved for offline use with the dictionary (`scripts/build-offline.js` saves every `.tsv` among the build's files).
- **Meanings come from the app's dictionary** when a list is added, like for a word typed by hand, so the "Dictionary meanings" setting applies. A list only carries a meaning for the few words the dictionary lacks (31 in HSK 3.0); those, with their readings, are hand-written in `data/wordlists/hsk/extra-meanings.tsv`. A test checks that every listed word can get a meaning.
- **Every listed word is matched to the dictionary** by its simplified form and reading (`bestMatch` in the build script) and takes its traditional form and reading from there, so that its ID is the same as when the word is typed by hand, and the same in both lists. The sources could not be used as they are: the HSK 3.0 source was matched to an older CC-CEDICT, and the HSK 2.0 source has character-by-character traditional forms (后面 for 後面) and tones as spoken (yi4qi3).
- **Do not use drkameleon/complete-hsk-vocabulary for readings.** It was the first source looked at; it lists every reading CC-CEDICT has for a word, not the one the HSK means (句 as gōu).
- **Seventeen TOCFL words had the wrong reading until 2026-10-13** (打 as dá, 累 as lěi, 個 as gě, 差, 吵, 混, 載 and others): a dictionary entry whose Taiwan reading fitted the list's pinyin counted as much as the entry that is read that way everywhere, and the first one won. `tocflFit` now gives an entry's own reading the edge, and where the list has no tone (個 "ge") the reading of the HSK list decides. It was found because a model writing notes for those words reported that the readings looked wrong: **a reading that looks odd in a list is worth checking against the committee's sheet** (the build script keeps a copy in `local/word-list-sources/`).
- **TOCFL** (`data/wordlists/tocfl/tocfl.tsv`, 7,433 words in seven levels) is built by the same script from the spreadsheet the test's committee publishes, read without any library (`filesInZip`, `sheetsOf`). The sheet has only traditional characters and tone-marked pinyin, with several things in a cell (你/妳, 爸爸/爸, 車(子), zhuyin notes); `formsOf` takes them apart and `tocflMatch` finds each word in the dictionary, also by its Taiwan reading. 79 entries the dictionary does not have that way are left out. **The source states no license**; the owner chose to include it, and the folder's README says so.
- **A word from a list is put in that level's deck**: its `decks` field gets the deck's ID, like `hsk-3.0/1` (`listTag` in `src/words.js`); a word already there gets it too. The deck records are made at the same time (`src/decks.js`). Until 2026-10-13 the field was called `lists`; `fileListWordsInDecks` in `src/main.js` converts it at every start.
- **A level can be divided into groups of words, each a deck inside the level** (asked for by the owner on 2026-10-13, as milestones smaller than a level). A list file's optional sixth column has the word's group, as its number within the level and its name (`03 Education 教育`); `listTag(listId, level, group)` makes the deck's ID from the group's number (`tocfl/1/03`), and `readyMadeDeck` makes the deck from the ID, with its name from the list file when the list is added (`groupName`; a deck made without the list at hand is called "Group 3"). So no table of groups is kept in the code, every device makes the same decks, and a group can be renamed without leaving people's decks behind (changed on 2026-10-13, before any tester: until then the ID held the name). A word carries its group's ID only; it counts for the level because that contains the group. Renumbering groups, or moving words between them, still changes things for people who have the list.
  - **TOCFL:** Novice 1 to Level 2 use the twelve topics of the committee's own sheets, in its order (`TOCFL_TOPICS` in the build script has the English names, which are Claude's translations). Levels 3 to 5 have no topics; level 3 is grouped like the HSK levels.
  - **HSK:** the lists are alphabetical inside a level. `withGroups` in the build script reads `data/wordlists/hsk/groups/<list>-<level>.tsv` (group number, group name, simplified, pinyin) where there is one, puts the level's words in that order and gives them their group. The owner's idea: a teaching order made by judgment, not by frequency, which would put the grammar words first. The owner approved the level 1 grouping on 2026-10-13. The files were written by Sonnet subagents to Claude's brief, checked by a script (every word once, groups of 12 to 40) and reread by Claude. They are under the MIT License (owner, 2026-10-13); the folder's README says how they were made.
  - **Group counts:** HSK 3.0 levels 1 to 6 have 28, 39, 47, 60, 70 and 78 groups; HSK 2.0 levels 1 to 5 have 11, 10, 17, 37 and 77; TOCFL level 3, which has no topics of the committee's, has 74 (`data/wordlists/tocfl/groups/`, read by the same `withGroups`). From level 4 up the owner has not read the groups; Claude read samples. The abstract vocabulary of the higher levels groups less cleanly: expect some loose groups of verbs and adjectives.
  - **Held back for their size** (2026-10-13): HSK 3.0 level 7-9 (5,634 words), HSK 2.0 level 6 (2,500), TOCFL levels 4 (2,267) and 5 (2,707). One agent sorted up to about 1,300 words well; twice that has not been tried. A word listed at two levels is kept at the lower one, so the build script reports such words as "not in" the higher level's group file; that is expected. A test checks that every word of a grouped level has a group.
  - **The build script keeps its sources** in the git-ignored `local/word-list-sources/` (since 2026-10-14, the owner's wish; until then `TOCFL_ZIP` pointed it at a copy of the TOCFL spreadsheet, whose server can take minutes). The first run on a computer downloads them; a rebuild then takes seconds.
- **Twenty-seven readings are put right by hand** (`data/wordlists/reading-fixes.tsv`, 2026-10-14), applied by the build script after matching. Matching by tones picks a rarer entry where the source writes a full tone that the everyday word reads neutral: the HSK source's shuōfǎ gave 说法 "to expound Buddhist teachings", 标致 was Peugeot; and the TOCFL's Taiwan full tones gave 告诉 "to press charges", 生意 "life force", 妻子 "wife and children". Five more were added the same day for the TOCFL: four words with a reading already judged wrong for HSK (说法, 得罪, 口音, 买卖; the first check only looked at a word once across all lists, so a list sharing a wrong reading with an earlier one was missed), and 著, matched to zhāo "a move in chess" because the dictionary gives that entry Taiwan's reading zhuó, where the sheet means zhuó "to wear, to apply". A correction takes the dictionary entry with the word's own traditional form. They were found by comparing each listed word with the dictionary's other entries of the same letters (95 differ from one only by a neutral tone; Claude read those and decided, the owner having left the unsure ones to Claude). When a correction changes a word's reading, the groups files and notes keyed by it are changed too. **A device that added such a word before is put right at start-up** (`applyReadingFixes` in `src/main.js`, `src/readingFixes.js`; asked for by the owner on 2026-10-14): a word in one of that list's decks under the wrong reading is renamed with `renameWord`, as if corrected in its pop-up panel, so its cards and reviews move with it and the old ID goes into `formerIds`. Its meaning is replaced only if it is still the dictionary's proposal for the wrong entry. A word typed in by hand is left alone (it may be meant), and so is one whose right reading the device has too. The dictionary is only fetched when there is something to correct. A word's reading stays part of its ID: the same characters are often two words (行 xíng and háng, 地道 dìdào "tunnel" and dìdao "authentic"; the HSK 3.0 list has 56 such), each with its own cards.
- **The dictionary now follows pointers.** Many CC-CEDICT entries only say "erhua variant of 一點" or "variant of 詞典", and were left out of the dictionary for having no meaning, so 一点儿 and 辞典 could not be found. `scripts/build-dictionary.js` now gives such an entry the meanings of the one it points to (7,142 entries; the dictionary has 124,481 and is 8.4 MB).

## Radicals

Built on 2026-10-14 at the owner's request. The answers marked "owner" were given to questions put to them; they have not tried it.

- **A fourth ready-made list, "Radicals 部首"**, in the Ready-made decks section, with two levels: **Common** and **Uncommon** (owner: all 214 Kangxi radicals, clearly separated, with the row's description saying what a new learner should start with). `READY_MADE_LISTS` in `src/decks.js` has it, so its decks are `radicals`, `radicals/Common`, `radicals/Uncommon`.
- **Common is decided by data**, not by hand: a radical is common if it is the radical of at least 10 characters of the HSK and TOCFL lists (Make Me a Hanzi's `radical` field, all forms of a radical counted together). That gives 80 radicals covering 88% of the lists' characters; the other 134 are Uncommon. Each level is most useful first. The 60 radicals with notes (`data/notes/radicals.tsv`) had been chosen from the lower levels only and are not the same set.
- **A radical that is a word of the lists, written the same way, is that word** (owner): 水, 口, 馬/马 have the same ID as when they come from the HSK or TOCFL list, so adding the deck puts a word already there into one more deck, with one schedule and all four kinds of card.
- **Any other form is a record of `kind: 'radical'`** (`src/radicals.js`), like a sentence is a word record of its own kind: ID `<traditional>|radical`, empty `pinyin`, its meaning from the list file. **Meaning and writing cards only** (owner), `RADICAL_CARD_TYPES`, enforced in `hasCardType` (`src/review.js`) and the card manager. Nothing speaks it: no loudspeaker in Review or on its page. It is taught in the Learn tab like a word ("New radical", four steps: no tones). Its pop-up panel changes only its meaning and decks. A list line with no reading is a radical (`entryId` in `src/words.js`).
- **A common radical brings each form that is the radical of at least 3 list characters** (心 and 忄, 金 and 釒/钅), and its main form if that is a word. Side forms that differ by script are one item (糹/纟, 釒/钅, 飠/饣, 言/讠). 230 items: 96 common, 134 uncommon.
- **Meanings of radicals that are not words** are hand-written in `data/wordlists/radicals/meanings.tsv`. A side form's meaning names the full form ("water (side form of 水)"), since writing prompts show only the meaning and 水 the word has the meaning "water". The build stops if a radical has none.
- **Built by `scripts/build-radicals.js`** (run by hand) from Unicode's `CJKRadicals.txt` (kept in `local/word-list-sources/`), Make Me a Hanzi and the word lists. The folder's README has the details (old Kangxi shapes replaced by today's; 阝 counted under 阜; 子 made a radical, since the lists only have the suffix zi).
- **Every radical that is not a word has a note** (`data/notes/radicals.tsv`, 156 since 2026-10-14: the owner wanted them all before the deck was pushed). A word's page shows its word note, not the radical's.
- **Not done:** the Chinese names of radicals (三點水); a radical's page listing the characters it is in.
