# The introduction, tips, menu, tabs and settings

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md on 2026-10-15; see `docs/README.md` for where each former section is now.

## The introduction (first use)

Asked for by the owner on 2026-10-13; the screens' wording and the extras noted below are Claude's.

- **Six screens in one `<dialog>`** (`welcome` in `index.html`, run by `src/welcome.js`): welcome; script and pronunciation; the four kinds of card, with a checkbox each; how it works; your words; keeping your data. Next and Back move between them; the last button ends in the Decks tab if there are no words yet.
- **Five screens since later on 2026-10-14:** "How it works" was taken out when the tips came (below); "Your words" says everything is in the menu and that each screen explains itself the first time.
- **Tips the first time each screen is opened** (the owner, 2026-10-14: the introduction is a wall of text many will not read; `src/tips.js`). A card at the bottom with one line and "Got it", which nothing waits for: Review, Learn, the first character written (in a lesson, a card or Free practice), the first character judged (`verdict`, announced by `src/wordFlow.js`: how to overrule a verdict), Decks, Ready-made decks, Words and a word's page. **One line each since 2026-10-15** (the owner chose the lines; tips of two or three sentences went unread in testing): each says one thing the screen does not already show, and a tip about one thing comes at the moment it first matters. Tips placed beside what they are about (a bubble pointing at a button) were left for the pass over the looks. The screens announce themselves with a `screen-shown` event on `document` and know nothing of tips. Each is shown once per device (`tipsSeen`; `tipsOn` switches them all, from a checkbox under "Help, feedback and licenses", with "Show all tips again" beside it; both settings belong to the device). The card is a popover, so it lies above a pop-up panel opened before it (a word's page); while the introduction is open, tips wait for it to close. **The browser tests switch tips off** at start, the way they choose the script (`openFreePractice` in the harness); `tests/browser/tips.browser.js` switches them back on.
- **Brought up to date on 2026-10-14** (asked for by the owner after the menu, the ready-made levels and the radicals): "How it works" names the menu and what is in it, says words are spoken (and where to change that), and mentions "I know this word"; **"Your words" offers a first level to start with** (`welcome-start`: HSK 3.0 level 1, TOCFL Novice 1, or nothing), suggested from the script and pronunciation chosen two screens before (traditional or Taiwan first suggests TOCFL; a device with words gets nothing) until it is chosen by hand. Finishing with a level adds it through the Decks tab's own code (`addReadyLevels`, which the panel hands out), with "Adding words…" on the button meanwhile, and ends in Learn; if the list cannot be fetched, it ends in Decks as before.
- **It opens by itself once per device**, while the `welcomeDone` setting is missing, so every existing device sees it once too. It starts from the settings as they are. Choices are saved only when the last screen is finished; "Skip the introduction" and Esc change nothing. "Show the introduction again" in Settings reopens it.
- **Long-term storage is only asked for after it has been explained.** The last screen says why, and its button calls `keepData.ask()` (`navigator.storage.persist()`, in `src/offline.js`). Settings has the same button under "Offline use" while the answer is no.
- **What the app says depends on the browser** (the owner asked for this on 2026-10-13): `browserKind` and `storageAdvice` in `src/storageAdvice.js`, plain calculation with tests. Firefox asks the person, so the app says to expect the question and, after a refusal, how to undo it. Chromium browsers never ask and say yes for an installed app, so the app says to install first, and after a no, that it will ask again by itself. Safari decides by itself and on an iPhone or iPad deletes a site's data after about a week unless the app is on the Home Screen, so the app says to put it there. Every refusal ends with "use Export backup".
- **"Install the app" button:** a Chromium browser announces `beforeinstallprompt` when the app can be installed; `src/offline.js` keeps the event and shows the button in the introduction and in Settings. Other browsers get the menu instructions in the advice.
- **Asking again without bothering anyone:** once the button has been tapped (`keepDataWanted` setting), browsers that decide by themselves are asked again at every start and when the app is installed (`appinstalled`), which shows nothing. Firefox is not, because it would put its question up again.
- **Not tried in Firefox or Safari:** the browser tests can only drive Chromium. The wording for the other two follows their documented behavior; the owner should try Firefox, which is installed on their computer.
- **The scripts and pronunciations are explained where they are chosen** (asked for by the owner on 2026-10-13), for someone new to Chinese: where each script is used and an example of a character that differs, with "Simplified" and "Traditional" linking to their Wikipedia articles, and a link to "Taiwanese Mandarin" for the pronunciation. The same text is in the introduction and behind a "?" beside Script and Pronunciation in Settings. Any `.help-button` in the settings panel shows the element named in its `aria-controls` (`src/settings.js`). The owner preferred one link per script over Wikipedia's "Debate on traditional and simplified Chinese characters", which is long and about the argument.
- **Beyond what was asked:** pronunciation is chosen on the same screen as the script, and the kinds of card can be switched off where they are explained.
- **It is opened before the first tab is shown** (`startUp` in `src/main.js`), which the browser tests rely on: `startBrowsers` skips it as soon as the app has started, since a `<dialog>` lies over the page and would catch the strokes. `{ keepWelcome: true }` leaves it, for `tests/browser/welcome.browser.js`.
- **The menu** (the owner, 2026-10-14, on two rows of buttons stacked at the top: until then Review, Learn, Decks and Words were tabs across the top and Settings a cog wheel in the upper right corner): a top bar with a menu button (☰, `menu-button`) and the name of the screen (`place-title`, from `PLACE_TITLES` in `src/main.js`), lined up with the content column. The button opens a panel from the left (`side-menu`, a `<dialog>`) with Review, Decks, Words and, under a line, Settings (Learn was in it until later that day; see "Learning and reviewing"); choosing anything closes it, as do Esc and a tap beside it. The buttons in it keep the IDs they had as tabs (`review-tab`, `decks-tab`, `settings-button`…), which the browser tests tap directly (`click()` works on a closed dialog's buttons). The backup reminder dot is on the menu button and on Settings in the panel, so it is plain where to go; the menu button is three bare lines, not a round button (owner). The footer only holds the "New version ready" button now. Above the list of words, "Show", "Order" and "Select words" share one row (`list-tools`).
- **What the review led to, built the same day at the owner's request** (they have not seen it):
  - **Four tabs: Review, Learn, Decks, Words.** The Decks tab shows the decks (choosing, adding to, studying one); the Words tab shows the words (a deck chooser, search, Show, Order, Select words, the list). Both are the one panel of `src/wordsPanel.js`, told which `view` it is (`show('decks')` or `show('words')`; modes `'words'` and `'browse'` in `src/main.js`, the first name being older than the split). The part the other tab shows is put out of view with the class `view-hidden`, not the `hidden` attribute, and is still kept up to date: on the Decks tab the list follows the open deck (`listDeck`). **Most browser tests read the list of words while the Decks tab is open**, which works for that reason; `tests/browser/tabs.browser.js` checks what is actually in view. **A deck's page lists its words under it** (the owner, 2026-10-14: it felt strange to open a deck and not see them; until then an "Its words" button opened the Words tab on that deck): on the Decks tab the words part is shown whenever a deck's page is open, without its first row (`words-tab-tools`: the deck chooser and Free practice), and is out of view on the front page.
  - **Free practice is no longer a tab.** Its button (still `practice-tab`, which the test harness taps) is in the Words tab, and a word's page has "Practice".
  - **Settings is in five groups that fold away** (`settings-group`): What you are learning (open), Studying, Meanings and sound, Your data and backups, "Help, feedback and licenses" (called Help until 2026-10-14, when the owner found that too narrow). The data group says when a backup was last made (`lastBackupAt`, a setting that belongs to the device) and opens by itself, with a dot on the menu button, when there are words and no backup for two weeks (`showBackupStatus` in `src/main.js`).
  - **A word's cards are on its page**: the "Cards" and "Suspended cards" fold-outs moved there from the pop-up panel, and ticking a suspended box takes effect at once (`showCardsOnPage` in `src/wordsPanel.js`). The panel is now only a form with Save. It keeps a word's `suspended` as stored.
  - **The app asks in its own panel** before removing, starting over or replacing (`ask` and `tell` in `src/confirm.js`, `confirm-dialog`), with buttons that say what they do. Tests answer it with `ANSWER_YES` from the harness.
  - **"I know this word" sits beside the lesson's note**, so a lesson's writing step fits a phone screen again; and a leech's note in Review has "Suspend this card", which takes the card out ungraded.
  - **Opening one of "Add a word", "Add a list of words", "Add a sentence" closes the others.**
  - **Not done:** one place for every message (each form still reports in its own spot); merging the pop-up panel into the word's page as an editing mode.
- **A review of the app's structure was done on 2026-10-13** (by Claude, from screenshots at phone size and counts; nobody else has reviewed it). Its findings, for whoever restructures:
  - The Decks tab does five jobs: moving between decks, adding things (three forms), finding words, managing cards (filters, order, selecting several) and the ready-made lists. A deck's page is over ten screens long with a hundred words on it.
  - Settings is one list of 27 controls, 2.7 phone screens long, under three headings; Export and Import backup are near its bottom although the data exists nowhere else.
  - One word has three surfaces: its page (to read), its pop-up panel (to change it, with its decks, cards and suspended cards) and two buttons on Review's answer page. In the panel some things act at once and others only on Save.
  - Free practice is a tab of its own although it is mainly for trying cards out.
  - On a 390 by 844 phone a lesson's writing step is 46px taller than the screen, so "I know this word" is below the fold.
  - To suspend a card that Review has just called a leech takes leaving Review and six steps.
  - Claude's advice was to stop adding features, publish what is built, and have the owner (and anyone else willing) study with it for a week or two before restructuring, since use will say which of these hurt.
- **Review's header was made shorter** (the owner, 2026-10-13: "overly tall and busy"): the deck chooser is narrow and shares a line with the counts, and "Show pinyin" is a link beside the kind of card instead of a button on a row of its own.
- **The word's page must not hand on a copy of a word from before it was changed.** It waits for two files before it is filled in; a word changed from the page meanwhile ("I know this word") used to be handed to the card manager afterwards as it was before, and "Start over" then saved it without `learnedAt`. `show` in `src/wordPage.js` now reads the word afresh and shows its cards before waiting for anything. It came to light as a test that failed one run in five.
- **A round button needs `min-height: 0`.** Every button is at least 48px tall (the `button` rule), so one given a width and a `border-radius` of 50% comes out oblong unless it says otherwise; `button.icon-button` does.
- **A pass over the app's looks is its own task, for when the features have reached what a version 1.0 needs** (the owner, 2026-10-13; until then only what is plainly broken is fixed, and looks are not otherwise worked on). It came up after several things looked off: oblong round buttons, a button stranded in the center, a drop-down that looked like a text box). Claude's advice was to do it as one piece of work together with dark mode, since both begin with gathering the colors and sizes into named values, and to look at every screen at phone, tablet and desktop width.
- **The app starts with no words.** Until 2026-10-13 a new database was given 109 test words. `removeOldBuiltInWords` in `src/main.js` removes them, studied or not, once from every device that was given them (it has the `builtInListVersion` setting): the owner asked for that. Their IDs are in `src/oldBuiltInWords.js`.
