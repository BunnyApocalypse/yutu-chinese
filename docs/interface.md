# The introduction, tips, menu, tabs and settings

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md on 2026-10-15; see `docs/README.md` for where each former section is now.

## The introduction (first use)

Asked for by the owner on 2026-10-13; the screens' wording and the extras noted below are Claude's.

- **Six screens in one `<dialog>`** (`welcome` in `index.html`, run by `src/welcome.js`): welcome; script and pronunciation; the four kinds of card, with a checkbox each; how it works; your words; keeping your data. Next and Back move between them; the last button ends in the Decks tab if there are no words yet.
- **Five screens since later on 2026-10-14:** "How it works" was taken out when the tips came (below); "Your words" says everything is in the menu and that each screen explains itself the first time.
- **Four short screens since 2026-10-16** (the owner, after a tester found the introduction far too wordy and the UX review found Next below the fold on a phone; the owner approved the draft): **Welcome** (one sentence and the look), **Which characters are you learning?** (the script; "What's the difference?" and "Pronunciation: …" are folds, `welcome-more`), **Select your starting level**, **Keep your words safe**. Each has one or two lines. The four kinds of card are no longer chosen here (all four stay on; Settings has them). **The pronunciation follows the script** until chosen by hand in its fold (`PRONUNCIATION_FOR_SCRIPT` in `src/welcome.js`: simplified → mainland only, traditional → Taiwan, either "both" → Accept both; the owner's choices). **The last screen has at most three buttons** (the tester's rule as a designer): Back, one main button that does what this browser needs, and "Not now" in place of "Skip the introduction", which saves the choices without asking. `welcomeStorage` in `src/storageAdvice.js` (tested) picks the main button and one line: "Install the app and start" where a Chromium browser offers its install question (it keeps only an installed app's data, and the app asks again by itself once installed), otherwise "Keep my words safe and start"; Firefox is told to choose "Allow", at the top of the page on a computer and at the bottom of the screen on a phone (the owner, 2026-10-16), and on a phone that Install in its menu gives an icon (the owner confirmed Firefox on Android asks too); an iPhone or iPad is told to add the app to the Home Screen. With storage already allowed or no way to ask, the button is "Start learning" as before and "Not now" goes. Neither question is waited for.
- **Tips, one line each, beside what they are about** (`src/tips.js`). First (the owner, 2026-10-14: the introduction is a wall of text many will not read) a card at the bottom of the screen with two or three sentences the first time each screen was opened; in testing they went unread. **Since 2026-10-15** (the owner chose the lines and asked for each tip to be paired with what it explains; they did not like tips that pop up at the bottom): each tip says one thing, comes when that thing first matters, and sits beside it with a small arrow pointing at it. The seven, in `TIPS` with their element and side: the grades (`grades`, when first offered, from `offerGrades` in `src/review.js`), "I know this word" (`lesson`, at a lesson's first step; it was the Learn tab's tip until then), the row of character boxes (`verdict`, when a character of a word of several is first judged, from `src/wordFlow.js`; one character has no boxes, its grade is its verdict), the gestures (`gestures`, below Restart, the first time a character can be written; since 2026-10-15, `docs/writing.md`), ☰ (`decks`), the first ready-made level's Add (`ready`), and the parts on a word's page (`wordPage`, below them so the character stays clear). **Taken out that day:** "Write anywhere in the box, at any size" (the owner: plain enough, with the animations) and the Words tab's tip (a word is what anyone would tap; Free practice is for the pass over the looks).
  - **A tip waits until its element can be seen**, and is put away while it cannot (another tab, scrolled away, a pop-up panel over it): `targetBox` asks the browser what is at the element's middle (`elementFromPoint`), which also covers a word page's own scrolling. A timer (`LOOK_EVERY_MS`) makes the tip follow its element and brings up a waiting one; it does nothing while no tip is up or waiting.
  - The screens announce moments with a `screen-shown` event on `document` and know nothing of tips; announcements without a tip are let pass. Each tip is shown once per device (`tipsSeen`; `tipsOn` switches them all, from a checkbox under "Help, feedback and licenses", with "Show all tips again" beside it; both settings belong to the device). The card is a popover, moved into the pop-up panel opened last (outside it nothing can be tapped); while the introduction is open, tips wait for it to close. **The browser tests switch tips off** at start, the way they choose the script (`openFreePractice` in the harness); `tests/browser/tips.browser.js` switches them back on.
- **Brought up to date on 2026-10-14** (asked for by the owner after the menu, the ready-made levels and the radicals): "How it works" names the menu and what is in it, says words are spoken (and where to change that), and mentions "I know this word"; **"Your words" offers a first level to start with** (`welcome-start`: HSK 3.0 level 1, TOCFL Novice 1, or nothing), suggested from the script and pronunciation chosen two screens before (traditional or Taiwan first suggests TOCFL; a device with words gets nothing) until it is chosen by hand. Finishing with a level adds it through the Decks tab's own code (`addReadyLevels`, which the panel hands out), with "Adding words…" on the button meanwhile, and ends in Learn; if the list cannot be fetched, it ends in Decks as before.
- **It opens by itself once per device**, while the `welcomeDone` setting is missing, so every existing device sees it once too. It starts from the settings as they are. Choices are saved only when the last screen is finished (its main button or "Not now"); "Skip the introduction" and Esc change nothing. "Show the introduction again" in Settings reopens it.
- **Long-term storage is only asked for after it has been explained.** The last screen says why, and its main button calls `keepData.ask()` (`navigator.storage.persist()`, in `src/offline.js`). Settings has the same button under "Offline use" while the answer is no.
- **What the app says depends on the browser** (the owner asked for this on 2026-10-13): `browserKind` and `storageAdvice` in `src/storageAdvice.js`, plain calculation with tests. Firefox asks the person, so the app says to expect the question and, after a refusal, how to undo it. Chromium browsers never ask and say yes for an installed app, so the app says to install first, and after a no, that it will ask again by itself. Safari decides by itself and on an iPhone or iPad deletes a site's data after about a week unless the app is on the Home Screen, so the app says to put it there. Every refusal ends with "use Export backup".
- **"Install the app" button:** a Chromium browser announces `beforeinstallprompt` when the app can be installed; `src/offline.js` keeps the event and shows the button in Settings (in the introduction it is the main button since 2026-10-16). The browser refuses the question unless something was just tapped; `install()` then does nothing. Other browsers get the menu instructions in the advice.
- **Said once more, after the first word learned** (`src/storageMessage.js`; the owner's choice on 2026-10-15, after a tester asked for a sign of how storage stands): on the screen between two words in Learn, if long-term storage is not allowed by then, a box under "Learn the next word" with the same browser's advice, "Install the app" where the browser offers it, "Allow long-term storage" and "Not now". Once per device (`storageMessageShown`, a setting of the device). Not a dot: Chrome only allows it to an installed app, so a dot would stay on for most people. Review announces the moment with `screen-shown` and `learned`; any other announcement, "Learn the next word" or "Not now" puts the box away, and it goes by itself once storage is allowed.
- **Asking again without bothering anyone:** once the button has been tapped (`keepDataWanted` setting), browsers that decide by themselves are asked again at every start and when the app is installed (`appinstalled`), which shows nothing. Firefox is not, because it would put its question up again.
- **Not tried in Firefox or Safari:** the browser tests can only drive Chromium. The wording for the other two follows their documented behavior; the owner should try Firefox, which is installed on their computer.
- **The scripts and pronunciations are explained where they are chosen** (asked for by the owner on 2026-10-13), for someone new to Chinese: where each script is used and an example of a character that differs, with "Simplified" and "Traditional" linking to their Wikipedia articles, and a link to "Taiwanese Mandarin" for the pronunciation. The same text is in the introduction and behind a "?" beside Script and Pronunciation in Settings. Any `.help-button` in the settings panel shows the element named in its `aria-controls` (`src/settings.js`). The owner preferred one link per script over Wikipedia's "Debate on traditional and simplified Chinese characters", which is long and about the argument.
- **Beyond what was asked:** pronunciation is chosen on the same screen as the script (since 2026-10-16 it follows the script, in a fold), and until that day the kinds of card could be switched off where they were explained.
- **It is opened before the first tab is shown** (`startUp` in `src/main.js`), which the browser tests rely on: `startBrowsers` skips it as soon as the app has started, since a `<dialog>` lies over the page and would catch the strokes. `{ keepWelcome: true }` leaves it, for `tests/browser/welcome.browser.js`.
- **The menu** (the owner, 2026-10-14, on two rows of buttons stacked at the top: until then Review, Learn, Decks and Words were tabs across the top and Settings a cog wheel in the upper right corner): a top bar with a menu button (☰, `menu-button`) and the name of the screen (`place-title`, from `PLACE_TITLES` in `src/main.js`), lined up with the content column. The button opens a panel from the left (`side-menu`, a `<dialog>`) with Review, Decks, Words and, under a line, Settings (Learn was in it until later that day; see "Learning and reviewing"; since 2026-10-16 it is back under Review while words are waiting, with how many, `docs/cards.md`); choosing anything closes it, as do Esc and a tap beside it. The buttons in it keep the IDs they had as tabs (`review-tab`, `decks-tab`, `settings-button`…), which the browser tests tap directly (`click()` works on a closed dialog's buttons). The backup reminder dot is on the menu button and on Settings in the panel, so it is plain where to go; the menu button is three bare lines, not a round button (owner). The footer only holds the "New version ready" button now. Above the list of words, "Show", "Order" and "Select words" share one row (`list-tools`).
- **What the review led to, built the same day at the owner's request** (they have not seen it):
  - **Four tabs: Review, Learn, Decks, Words.** The Decks tab shows the decks (choosing, adding to, studying one); the Words tab shows the words (a deck chooser, "My words only", search, Show, Order, Select words, the list, and while searching the dictionary's words under yours: `docs/words.md`). Both are the one panel of `src/wordsPanel.js`, told which `view` it is (`show('decks')` or `show('words')`; modes `'words'` and `'browse'` in `src/main.js`, the first name being older than the split). The part the other tab shows is put out of view with the class `view-hidden`, not the `hidden` attribute, and is still kept up to date: on the Decks tab the list follows the open deck (`listDeck`). **Most browser tests read the list of words while the Decks tab is open**, which works for that reason; `tests/browser/tabs.browser.js` checks what is actually in view. **A deck's page lists its words under it** (the owner, 2026-10-14: it felt strange to open a deck and not see them; until then an "Its words" button opened the Words tab on that deck): on the Decks tab the words part is shown whenever a deck's page is open, without its first row (`words-tab-tools`: the deck chooser and Free practice), and is out of view on the front page.
  - **Free practice is no longer a tab.** Its button (still `practice-tab`, which the test harness taps) is in the Words tab, and a word's page has "Practice".
  - **Settings is in six groups** (`settings-group`), as regrouped on 2026-10-15 (the owner: Look under "Studying" made no sense, and "What you are learning" sounded awkward): **Chinese** (`settings-learning`: script, pronunciation, card types, sentences, dictionary meanings: what is learned and tested), **Daily study** (`settings-studying`: new words per day, teaching before testing in Review, leeches), **Writing** (`settings-writing`: handwriting, the stroke count in Review, how a hint counts), **Look and sound** (`settings-look`: the look as the same three samples as the introduction, sound, volume), Your data and backups, "Help, feedback and licenses" (called Help until 2026-10-14, when the owner found that too narrow). Before, there were five: What you are learning, Studying (which held most of it), Meanings and sound, and the last two. The data group says when a backup was last made (`lastBackupAt`, a setting that belongs to the device); when there are words and no backup for two weeks there is a dot on the menu button and on the data group's row in Settings (`showBackupStatus` in `src/main.js`; until 2026-10-16 the group opened by itself). **With no backup yet** (UX review, 2026-10-16: a red dot in a new learner's first minutes looked like an error), the dot waits until a day after the first card was studied, or a week if the browser has promised to keep the data (`navigator.storage.persisted()`): the owner asked whether a week was safe, and Safari clears a site not opened for 7 days. While the dot is on, a line under Settings in the menu says what it is (`backup-due-line`), since a phone cannot show the menu button's title. **Settings' heading and "Done" stay at the top** while it scrolls (`settings-heading`; UX review, 2026-10-16); the "Done" at its bottom was taken out the same day (the owner).

The browser tests open a group with `openSettings(page, group)` in the harness, since text in a hidden group reads as empty.

- **A review of the app's structure was done on 2026-10-13** (by Claude, from screenshots at phone size and counts; nobody else has reviewed it). Its findings, for whoever restructures:
  - The Decks tab does five jobs: moving between decks, adding things (three forms), finding words, managing cards (filters, order, selecting several) and the ready-made lists. A deck's page is over ten screens long with a hundred words on it.
  - Settings is one list of 27 controls, 2.7 phone screens long, under three headings; Export and Import backup are near its bottom although the data exists nowhere else.
  - One word has three surfaces: its page (to read), its pop-up panel (to change it, with its decks, cards and suspended cards) and two buttons on Review's answer page. In the panel some things act at once and others only on Save.
  - Free practice is a tab of its own although it is mainly for trying cards out.
  - On a 390 by 844 phone a lesson's writing step is 46px taller than the screen, so "I know this word" is below the fold.
  - To suspend a card that Review has just called a leech takes leaving Review and six steps. (No longer: "Suspend this card" under the grades makes it one, as the UX review of 2026-10-16 found.)
  - Claude's advice was to stop adding features, publish what is built, and have the owner (and anyone else willing) study with it for a week or two before restructuring, since use will say which of these hurt.
- **Review's header was made shorter** (the owner, 2026-10-13: "overly tall and busy"): the deck chooser is narrow and shares a line with the counts, and "Show pinyin" is a link beside the kind of card instead of a button on a row of its own.
- **The word's page must not hand on a copy of a word from before it was changed.** It waits for two files before it is filled in; a word changed from the page meanwhile ("I know this word") used to be handed to the card manager afterwards as it was before, and "Start over" then saved it without `learnedAt`. `show` in `src/wordPage.js` now reads the word afresh and shows its cards before waiting for anything. It came to light as a test that failed one run in five.
- **A round button needs `min-height: 0`.** Every button is at least 48px tall (the `button` rule), so one given a width and a `border-radius` of 50% comes out oblong unless it says otherwise; `button.icon-button` does.
- **A pass over the app's looks is its own task, for when the features have reached what a version 1.0 needs** (the owner, 2026-10-13; until then only what is plainly broken is fixed, and looks are not otherwise worked on). It came up after several things looked off: oblong round buttons, a button stranded in the center, a drop-down that looked like a text box). Claude's advice was to do it as one piece of work together with dark mode, since both begin with gathering the colors and sizes into named values, and to look at every screen at phone, tablet and desktop width. **The groundwork is in `docs/ux-review.md`** (2026-10-16): the screenshot atlas (`node scripts/ux-atlas.js`), what it measured, findings ranked by severity, and the steps the common tasks take.
- **Dark mode** (asked for by the owner on 2026-10-13, built on 2026-10-15): the "Look" setting (`theme`, a setting of the device) in Settings › Look and sound and on the first screen of the introduction: "Follow device theme" (the default: the device's own preference where the browser can tell, light otherwise), Light or Dark. In the introduction the three are samples side by side, made from `icons/swatch.png` (the icon's paper, grid and 兔 in dark ink, without the night sky; until 2026-10-15 the icon itself, its handwritten 字 on the grid): as it is, turned dark with a CSS filter, and the two stacked and cut along the diagonal (the owner chose the icon and the diagonal; a split of plain text down the middle came apart in some fonts), the radio buttons kept out of sight for the keyboard and screen readers (the owner, 2026-10-15, who found three radio buttons with "As this device is set" poor). **Both looks are soft** (the owner, 2026-10-15, after trying beige paper and dark gray on the samples): paper (`--surface` #fbf8f1) rather than white and dark gray text and ink (#34322d) rather than black in the light look; a warm dark gray page (#23221f) and softer light text in the dark one; the samples are filtered to match. **Every color is named once at the top of `src/style.css`**, with a value for each look (`:root`, `:root[data-theme='dark']`, and the same dark values under `prefers-color-scheme` for "Follow device theme"); the rest of the style sheet only uses `var(--name)`. `src/theme.js` puts `data-theme` on the page, or none for "Follow device theme", so the style sheet follows the device by itself from the first moment, before the settings are read. Hanzi Writer needs colors as values: `src/quiz.js` reads them with `themeColor` and sets them again on `theme-changed`; your kept strokes and the grid use `var(--ink)` and `var(--grid)` as styles and follow by themselves. In the introduction a look is shown as it is chosen and saved at the end like the rest; skipping puts the saved one back. The manifest's colors stay light (a manifest has one); the `theme-color` the phone's browser bar takes follows the look. `tests/browser/settings.browser.js` checks the three choices with the device set either way.
- **Accessibility** (review asked for by the owner on 2026-10-15; findings 1 to 6 fixed that day at their word, and keys 1 to 4 for the grades added the same day, see `docs/cards.md`). What the app now does, and the rules for new code:
  - **Chinese is marked as Chinese** with `lang` (`zh-Hant-TW` or `zh-Hans-CN`, `langFor` in `src/lang.js`), or a screen reader reads it with an English voice and the phone may pick the wrong regional font. Text that mixes English and Chinese (a deck's name, a meaning, a note, "simp. 简体") goes through `fillMixed`, which marks each run of characters. Where the script is not known (a deck's name, a note), the learner's `settings.script` is taken. A row of a list marks each script's part (`scriptsShown` in `src/words.js`). Not marked: options of a drop-down list and text boxes, which cannot mark part of their text.
  - **Messages are read out:** the status line under the writing area (`#status`, not the stroke count, or every stroke would be read) and the notes after an action (adding words, importing, the leech notice, "All done") have `role="status"`.
  - **The menu marks the place shown** with `aria-current="page"` beside the class `selected` (`setMode` in `src/main.js`).
  - **A cut-off meaning can be opened by keyboard**: while it has more, `clampWithPopup` makes it a button for Tab, Enter and Space.
  - **Contrast:** text at least 4.5 to 1 (3 to 1 when large), in both looks; the edge of a text box or drop-down list 3 to 1 (`--field-border`, darker than `--border`). Green as text is `--success-text`; white text on red is on `--danger-fill`.
  - **Everything tapped is at least 24px by 24px** (WCAG 2.2, AA). A small button whose row must not grow ("I know this word", "About", "Edit", the words of a note or sentence) gets an invisible layer reaching 4px above and below it (`::after` in `src/style.css`) instead of padding. Words inside a line of text may be as narrow as the word.
  - **`tests/browser/accessibility.browser.js` checks all of the above** on each screen (Free practice, Decks, a deck's page, the menu, Words, a word's page, a lesson, Review with its grades, Settings, the introduction) in both looks. A new screen or pop-up should be added to it.
  - Not done: the 44px Apple and Google recommend (most buttons are 32 to 40px; the menu button and the loudspeaker are 36); writing itself cannot be done without drawing, by the nature of the app; checking by hand with VoiceOver or TalkBack.
- **The app starts with no words.** Until 2026-10-13 a new database was given 109 test words. `removeOldBuiltInWords` in `src/main.js` removes them, studied or not, once from every device that was given them (it has the `builtInListVersion` setting): the owner asked for that. Their IDs are in `src/oldBuiltInWords.js`.
