# Writing a character: grading, shortcuts, frame, stroke orders

## How the must-haves are implemented

- **Raw input or snap to print** (the `rawInput` setting, on by default; the owner uses raw input, the other mode is for people who prefer tidy characters). With it off, each correct stroke is replaced by the printed stroke as Hanzi Writer does on its own: the printed strokes are shown in ink while writing, no own strokes are kept, and the character is not followed when written off-center, since the printed strokes are a visible reference. Everything else (shortcuts, both stroke orders, verdicts) is the same in both modes. `colorWhileWriting` and `referenceColor` in `src/quiz.js` hold the difference.
- **Raw input:** Hanzi Writer's printed strokes are kept invisible during a quiz (`strokeColor` fully transparent). Each accepted stroke's `drawnPath` is drawn into our own SVG layer (`g.drawn-strokes`) in `src/quiz.js`. A rejected stroke fades away and counts as a mistake. On completion the printed character appears underneath in a light color for comparison.
- **Shortcuts:** Hanzi Writer always gets the unmodified textbook data, so display, animation and textbook-form grading are untouched. Both forms must be accepted: the shortcut is an option, never a requirement. `src/shortcuts.js` works out from the stroke data which pairs of consecutive strokes may be joined, and checks whether a drawn stroke is such a joined pair. `src/quiz.js` runs that check on every stroke Hanzi Writer reports and, on a match, keeps the stroke and advances past both textbook strokes with `writer.skipQuizStroke()`. Because Hanzi Writer has already counted such a stroke as a mistake, `quiz.js` keeps its own mistake count. Do not implement shortcuts by editing the stroke data handed to Hanzi Writer: that was tried, and it forces the shortcut form on everyone.
  - **Joinable pair** (`isJoinable`): the first stroke ends where the second begins (after at most a short lead-in), both parts are long enough to matter (145 units: the owner, 2026-11-01, the ノ of 亟 in 極 at 149 joined to the ㇆ after it; or 115 when the shorter is at least half the longer: the small 辶 of 隨, the owner, 2026-10-24), and the joined shape differs visibly from either stroke alone. There are no per-character lists. **The distances scale with the frame** (`scale`, from `drewShortcut` in `src/quiz.js`): whether a pair may be joined belongs to the character, not to how big it is written (the owner, 2026-10-24: the top of 辶 in 這 written at two thirds of the size could never be joined; their recording of raw input showed it).
  - **The error that matters is a misfire:** taking a single stroke for the joined pair. If it is the first stroke, the app skips a stroke that was never written; if it is the second, a forgotten first stroke passes unnoticed. A missed joined stroke only costs a mistake. `isShortcutStroke` therefore compares shapes with size taken away (a first stroke drawn too big reaches as far as the joined pair would), requires the joined reading to be clearly closer than either single stroke, and checks where the stroke starts.
  - `tests/shortcuts.test.js` simulates shifted, resized and shaky strokes over the test word list and requires zero misfires of both kinds. Run it after touching any constant in `src/shortcuts.js`.
  - **Out of scope so far:** three or more strokes in one go, and shortcuts that reorder or drop strokes (口 in 2, 糸's lower part), except 艹 in 3 (below).
- **艹 in three strokes** (the owner, 2026-10-30, writes it as fonts show it: one level stroke across, then the two uprights; Taiwan's is 丨 一 一 丨, two 十). Accepted as well as the four; the app still shows four.
  - **Found in the stroke data by shape** (`grassTopsOf` in `src/shortcuts.js`): four strokes in a row, an upright crossing a level stroke, a level stroke going on from it (within 150 units) crossed by an upright, and no other stroke reaching above the level strokes between their ends. An upright may stand up to 60 units past its level stroke's end instead (`UPRIGHT_PAST_END`; the owner, 2026-10-31: 觀 wasn't found, its right upright being just left of the right half; 警 and 護 likewise). **The 十 十 of 垂 is taken the same way** (the owner, 2026-11-02: 郵 was asked in four): where a 丨 runs down between the two halves, from above them to below, between the uprights (`runsDownBetween`), strokes above don't matter. That adds 郵, 睡 and the lower part of 華, and nothing else in either set (without the 丨, dropping the rule would add 興 and 飆 in the big set). In the Taiwan set 27 characters (花 茶 草 藍 萬 懂 臟 貓 髒 觀 郵 睡…; `tests/shortcuts.test.js` lists them), 華 with two; none in the big set, whose 艹 has three strokes or four in another order.
  - **While writing** (`src/quiz.js`, section "艹 in three strokes"): a stroke refused where the left upright or the left half is expected, that fits both halves joined (`levelFits`), counts for both halves; they are moved to that place in `sequence`, and the uprights come after (`acceptGrassLevel`, `grassJoined`). Written after the left upright, Hanzi Writer accepts the level stroke as the left half, so an accepted left half that ends nearer the right half's end and fits the joined halves skips the right half too (`isGrassLevelAccepted`). The first stroke of all may be anywhere; while the size is not known (`sizeIsKnown`), it is fitted at any size with the strokes before it (`sizeThatFits`, shared with `isExpectedStrokeAtAnotherSize`), and taken for the level stroke only if that fits better (`misfit`) than the left half alone at some size: written small, the left half is the whole level stroke at a quarter of the size (the four-stroke 藍 at half size failed so). Which stroke order the rest was in is told with the 艹 put back in four (`sequenceWithoutGrassJoins`).
  - **The big set's 艹 in 丨 一 丨 一** (the owner, 2026-11-04: 薦's 艹 couldn't be written at all). The big set writes the four-stroke 艹 of its traditional characters as one 十 after the other, which nobody writes and where the three-stroke 艹 isn't found. When writing traditional, `loadCharData` hands such a character over in the Taiwan order, 丨 一 一 丨, with the data's own order as `altOrder` (`inTaiwanGrassOrder`, `crossByCrossGrassOf` in `src/shortcuts.js`): 一 丨 丨, 丨 一 一 丨 and 丨 一 丨 一 are all accepted, and the animation shows the Taiwan order. Found as a 艹 once its last two strokes are swapped, its left level stroke beginning well left of its upright (the top of 叟 in 嫂 and 艘 and of 臿 in 插 has level strokes beginning at the uprights). 111 characters not in the Taiwan set (薦 夢 舊 蓋 蘭 驚 錨 葉 蔥…); simplified is untouched.
  - **Tested** in the writing grid: 草, 花, 藍, 觀, 郵, 華 and 薦 with 艹 level-first and upright-first, at every position and size, besides the four-stroke form.
  - **Limits:** the uprights before the level stroke (丨 丨 一) are not taken. The left half written first, out of order, as the first stroke of all passes for the level stroke (nothing yet says how big the character is); the next stroke then shows the mistake.
- **Leniency:** `USUAL_LENIENCY` in `src/quiz.js`, **1.8 since 2026-11-04** (the owner's choice after studying at it; 1.5 before; Hanzi Writer's own is 1). Every check of a stroke in `quiz.js` uses the value in force (`leniencyNow`), Hanzi Writer's and the app's own, so all loosen together. **Settings › For testing › "How strict the grading of strokes is"** (the owner, 2026-10-31, to find by studying what feels right rather than by measuring: "generally less strict strokes") chooses it on one device: 1.5 to 1.7, 1.8 (usual), 1.9, 2, 2.2, 2.5 (`LENIENCY_CHOICES` in `src/testerSettings.js`, `setLeniency`), from the next character. A raw input recording notes the value with each character (`leniency`). Measured on 2026-10-24 (`local/strictness/README.md`): at the usual size almost no right stroke is refused, and about 38% of another stroke of the same character, drawn well, already passes in place of the one asked, so higher lets more strokes written out of place through. **Measured for the change to 1.8** (2026-11-04, `measure.mjs compare`, a simulated hand over the Taiwan set; the big set a little lower): the right stroke refused at full size 0.6% → 0.3% (a sloppy hand 5.3% → 3.6%), at half size 4.7% → 3.0% (20.7% → 15.8%); the next stroke written in place of the one asked passes 4.3% → 6.9% at full size, 9.1% → 11.9% at half size. The app's own checks (joins, the other order, second shapes, steep dots) take no later stroke for the one asked at 1.8 either. In the real app a straight stroke up the middle of 滿 passes for the 提 of 氵 at 1.8 (`gestures.browser.js` now draws it at the right).

## Following where a character is written

- **The problem:** with raw input the reference is invisible, so the owner writes wherever and at whatever size feels natural, but Hanzi Writer grades against one fixed, centered position. A character begun off-center had its later strokes expected in the wrong place.
- **The fix** (`src/frame.js`, and the section of the same name in `src/quiz.js`): after each accepted stroke, a least-squares fit over all accepted strokes gives a *frame* (shift and size). If it has changed enough, Hanzi Writer is handed the stroke data moved into that frame and the quiz resumes, the same way the second stroke order is handled.
- **The data Hanzi Writer holds is always: base data, in the current stroke order, in the current frame** (`currentData` in `src/quiz.js`). Anything that compares drawn strokes with textbook ones must use the framed data (`framedData`, `charData`), never `baseData`, or it will judge against the centered position.
- **Because the data itself is moved**, hints appear where the character is being written, and the printed character at the end lines up with the handwriting.
- **Rules:** shift from the first stroke; size only once the strokes written span about 20% of the box; size limited to 0.55–1.5 (`MIN_SCALE`, `MAX_SCALE`) and shift to about 30% of the box; no following while the outline is shown or when raw input is off; Clear, Animate and a new character go back to the centered data.
- **The size is never taken from one stroke** (`MIN_STROKES_FOR_SCALE` in `src/frame.js`; the owner, 2026-10-24): the first stroke says where the character is, the second how big. Before, a long first stroke gave the size alone, so 鼎 begun where 臼 was asked (and 足 for 彳) had its shorter first stroke taken for a 臼 written at 0.4 of the box, and every later stroke was expected inside it, seen only with Show outline. Measured: after that first stroke the app followed at 0.40 to 0.60, now at full size; honest writing, small or not, is refused no more often (`local/strictness/README.md`). At the parting of two stroke orders the other order's stroke is measured at the size the new stroke gives (`otherOrderFor`), or 忙 begun small in the mainland order was refused.
- **The first stroke may land anywhere, at any size that can be followed.** If Hanzi Writer rejects the very first stroke only for where it is or how small, `isFirstStrokeAnywhere` in `src/quiz.js` accepts it by shape, and the frame follows. The owner writes at 0.6 to 1.1 of the box, often in a corner, so `fitsStroke` lets a first stroke be as short as 0.45 times the textbook's (0.5 for later strokes; 0.35 until 2026-11-08).
- **Which order a first stroke belongs to is decided by shape, not position** (`otherOrderFor`, when nothing is written yet). Written small in a corner, the mainland order's first stroke can lie nearer the MOE order's (長, 馬). **Only where the two shapes are as good as the same** (within `SAME_SHAPE_MARGIN`, 0.08, in `src/quiz.js`) does position decide (the owner, 2026-10-25: both of 學's first strokes are a ノ, and theirs at the left was read as 爻's). A wrong guess is still sorted out a stroke later by the re-reading described under "Two accepted stroke orders".
- **The brush's entry is not asked for** (`withoutEntry` in `src/strokeMatch.js`; the owner's recording of 學, 2026-10-25). Many Taiwan-set strokes begin with a short dab down or down-right before turning down-left or down (about 22% of the Taiwan set's strokes, 2,363 of 10,561); the app's own checks (`fitsStroke`, `shapeDifferenceFromStroke`: the other order, a first stroke anywhere, a stroke at another size) compare the shape with and without it. The ノ at the left of 學's 臼 runs at 62° with it and 41° without; the owner's ran at about 40° and were all refused as the mainland order's first stroke, so 學 in the mainland order took many tries. Recognized by direction and share of the stroke, not length, since the stroke may be shrunk into a frame. Measured over the Taiwan set: pairs of different strokes in a character whose shapes pass as each other rose by 1.3%. Hanzi Writer's own grading is unchanged.
- **Not changed (the owner to decide):** the same recording showed two more things. The 冖 written narrower than printed (its 乛 about two thirds of the printed length) is refused once or twice by Hanzi Writer's own grading. The left wing written at about 0.6 of the size of 爻 (try #0) gives a frame that no stroke of 爻 then fits, since one frame covers the whole character.
- **The size is not known after one short stroke** (`MIN_SPAN_FOR_SCALE`), so until the strokes span enough, the next ones are expected at full size around the first. Written at half size, the second or third stroke then lands too close to the first and Hanzi Writer rejects it. `isExpectedStrokeAtAnotherSize` in `src/quiz.js` catches that: taking the rejected stroke as the expected one, it fits a frame to it and the strokes before it, and accepts the stroke if all of them sit on their references at that size (judged by `fitsStroke`, more strictly the smaller the size). It only applies until the size is known (`sizeIsKnown` in `src/frame.js`). `otherOrderFor` counts the rejected stroke into its frame in the same way, or a character re-read into the other order would still be expected at full size.
- **Nothing is taken for a character smaller than 0.55 of the box** (`MIN_SCALE` in `src/frame.js`; the owner, 2026-11-08: a stroke written for another character, wrong for the one asked, was accepted as the right stroke of a tiny character). The owner's recordings show them writing at 0.61 to 1.08 of the box. **A fit that stops at the smallest size is refused, not judged there** (`sizeThatFits` in `src/quiz.js`): it means the strokes would fit only a still smaller character, and judging them at 0.55 would let more through, since less is allowed the smaller the character. Measured with the app's own code and Hanzi Writer's grading (the model hand of `local/strictness/measure.mjs`, both stroke data sets, leniency 1.8): with the first stroke written right at 0.75 of the box, stroke 2 of another common character passed as the asked one's second stroke in 7,077 pairs before and 4,925 after (中 asked, then 和's level stroke: taken for 中's ㇕ at 0.4). Per stroke 2 or 3, a wrong stroke passing as "written small": 2.6% → 2.1% for a writer at 0.6–0.75 of the box, little change from 0.75 up (most wrong strokes that pass fit at a normal size, which this cannot catch). Honest writing, characters with a right stroke refused: 9.1% → 10.7% at 0.6–0.75, unchanged from 0.75 up; at 0.5–0.6, 19.6% → 22%. Smallest sizes of 0.5 and 0.6 were measured too (0.5 hardly helped; 0.6 sits at the owner's smallest). The first stroke's shortest length went from 0.35 to 0.45 with it (no change for writing at 0.6 or more). `writing-grid.browser.js`: "a wrong stroke is not taken for the right one written tiny"; the grid's half-size positions became one at 0.65, bottom right.
- **A character begun too big is zoomed out** (2026-10-18; a beginner twice wrote the 金 of 鋼 so big the rest had no room; the owner chose zooming out over capping the frame, since a squeezed right part would be rejected for its shape). Once the size is known, after each accepted stroke, **if the next stroke would reach beyond the writing area** (`strokeLeavesRoom` in `src/frame.js`, its printed outline against `ROOM`: the box and its whole margin, to the area's edge), `zoomToFit` shrinks everything until the whole character (`extentOf`) fits with a little to spare (`ZOOM_SLACK`): the strokes on screen slide there in 0.2 s (`zoomInk` in `src/quiz.js`, `.drawn-strokes > g`; at once with reduced motion), keeping their line width (`vector-effect`), and the frame and accepted points with them (`useFrame`). **It shrinks toward the middle of the box, not toward where there is room**, so the slide hints as little as possible where the rest goes (the owner's worry); it shifts only if that isn't enough. `data-zoom` on the writing area is for the browser tests (`writing-grid.browser.js`, "begun too big"). **Until 2026-10-24 it zoomed as soon as the whole character would not fit, in half the margin**; the owner found strokes zoomed too often, and measured over both stroke data sets (`local/strictness/` in the main checkout, git-ignored) that caught one character in five written at exactly full size, since the size is guessed from the first few strokes. Waiting for the next stroke, with the whole margin: about one in a hundred, while a character really begun too big is still zoomed.
- **Limits:** only shift and size, not slant or stretch. A wrong stroke accepted early skews the frame for the strokes after it.

## Two accepted stroke orders

- **MOE order is the standard the app teaches.** The Taiwan set's strokes are in the order of Taiwan's Ministry of Education, which differs from the mainland order for 83 of its characters (學 starts with 爻; 我, 忙, 馬, 國, 錢 and others differ too). The owner is used to the mainland order for some of these, so **both are accepted** in Traditional mode, with no penalty.
- **Finding the second order:** `scripts/strokeOrder.js` compares each Taiwan-set character with the big set when the data is prepared. If both have the same strokes (matched strictly, both ways) in a different sequence, the mainland sequence is stored in the character's file as `altOrder`. When in doubt nothing is stored and only the MOE order applies.
- **Following it during a quiz** (`src/quiz.js`, section "Two accepted stroke orders"): Hanzi Writer holds one order and grades strictly in sequence. When it rejects a stroke that fits what the other order expects next (`src/strokeMatch.js`), the stroke is accepted, Hanzi Writer is handed the same strokes rearranged via `setCharacter()`, and the quiz resumes with `quizStartStrokeNum`. Raw input make the swap invisible.
- **Which order is meant can be unclear at first, so the strokes written are re-read when needed.** 學 starts with a ノ in either order (in the middle for MOE, at the left for mainland), and with an invisible reference and a movable frame the first stroke fits both. The owner's screencast showed the result: the mainland first stroke was taken as MOE's, and the next stroke was then rejected forever. Two checks in `src/quiz.js` handle it, both built on `otherReadingOf`, which re-reads the strokes written since the two orders last agreed (`lastSyncPoint`) as the other order's:
  - `otherOrderFor`, on a rejected stroke: switch if that stroke and the earlier ones all fit the other order, in the frame that reading implies.
  - `otherOrderBeats`, on an accepted stroke: switch if the other reading fits clearly better (the 丨 of the left part can pass for a stroke of 爻 when written over it).
  Neither applies at the last stroke of a character.
- **A rejected stroke can pass both as a shortcut and as the other order's next stroke.** Written to the right, the left 丨 of 學 runs alongside the 丶 and ノ of the 爻 joined up. `otherOrderIsCloser` decides by which of the two the stroke lies nearer to; before 2026-10-13 the shortcut always won.
- **No mixing inside a differing stretch:** changing order is only offered where the strokes written so far are a complete beginning of both orders (`remainingInOtherOrder`). A character with two separate differing stretches (錢) may use a different order in each.
- **Clear and Animate always return to the MOE order.**
- **Writing during Animate stops it, and the stroke counts** (`catchStroke` in `src/quiz.js`; the owner, 2026-10-18: "the user should be allowed to write and interrupt the animation"), also during the pause after it (`animating`). The touch starts the quiz (`start`). Hanzi Writer only listens once its quiz runs, so the touch's points are kept, and if the browser's own `mousedown`/`touchstart` didn't reach it (`heard`), they are handed to it as mouse events. **Starting a quiz doesn't call off Hanzi Writer's animation**: its end still arrives, and is ignored once anything else has started (it used to restart the quiz 1.5 s later, wiping strokes). Tested both ways in `gestures.browser.js`.
- **No note when the mainland order is used** (the owner, 2026-10-25: the order is accepted, so nothing needs saying). Until then a line under the buttons pointed out that the MOE order differs.
- Reviews store `orderPerChar`: `'moe'`, `'mainland'`, `'other'` (a 方 with its ノ first, below), or `null` for characters with only one order.
- **The big set's 忄 in the Taiwan order too** (the owner, 2026-11-06: 愉 refused 丶 丨 丶 while 快 took it). The big set writes 忄 丶 丶 丨 and has only that order; when writing traditional, `loadCharData` adds 丶 丨 丶 as `altOrder`, marked `altOrderIsTaiwan` so a review's `orderPerChar` is the right way round (`startsWithMainlandHeart`, `inTaiwanOrder` in `src/shortcuts.js`). Found only as the first three strokes (two short strokes going down either side of a tall upright, the right one going right): 157 characters not in the Taiwan set (愉 悅 惱 懶 憶…); further in, the same test finds shapes that are no 忄 (赧, 椭). **The data's own order stays first, unlike 艹**: the orders then part at the long upright, which `otherOrderFor` tells apart well; handed over the other way, the mainland right dot written small was judged by `fitsStroke`, stricter on the shape of so small a stroke than Hanzi Writer, and refused now and then (憶, 懶 in the grid). 懞, with a 艹 too, has the 艹 in the Taiwan order and a second order mixing the two. The writing grid writes 愉.
- **垂 with its 丨 after the levels, in both scripts** (the owner, 2026-11-10, in 睡: both sets force the long 丨 before the middle level stroke and the two short strokes). Accepted: ノ, 一, the middle level stroke, the short stroke each side, the next level stroke, the 丨, the bottom level stroke. `chuiOf` in `src/shortcuts.js` finds it by shape (睡 and 郵 in the Taiwan set, nine strokes with the middle level in two halves; 睡 錘 捶 陲 唾 and others in the big set, eight strokes); `withChuiUprightLast` adds the order as `altOrder` (marked `altOrderIsFang` when alone, so a review says 'other'), joining one already there as the 方 does. The middle level written across both halves (the 艹 join) is accepted there too: `grassLevelFor` in `src/quiz.js` takes it where the 丨 is expected (`isChuiTop`) and `acceptGrassLevel` then expects the uprights, the next level stroke, the 丨. The 丨 right after the joined level stroke is not accepted. The writing grid writes 睡 all three ways.
- **The branch of 匕 right to left** (the owner, 2026-11-10, in 北): the Taiwan set's first stroke of 匕 rises to the right; the big set's ノ was already a second shape, but a flatter stroke going left fitted neither. `branchesOf` finds it by shape (a short rising level stroke starting at the 丨 of the hook after it: 北 比 化 死 能 花 and a few more, 24 in the Taiwan set), `withReversedBranches` adds `reversedShapes` (the stroke run backwards, and that tilted 28° down at the left), tried by `otherShapeFor` after `altShapes`. The big set's data needs none (its first stroke is the ノ). Both are applied by `withMoreWays` in `src/charData.js`; 巴 and 包 are found too, harmlessly.
- **方 with its ノ before its ㇆, in both scripts** (the owner, 2026-11-09, after a native writer confirmed writing it so). Both sets write 丶 一 ㇆ ノ; `loadCharData` adds the order with the last two swapped as `altOrder`, wherever 方 stands (放, 房, 旁, 訪, 旅, 族, 遊, 激…), in simplified too (`fangsOf`, `withFangInEitherOrder` in `src/shortcuts.js`). Found by shape: a short dot over a level stroke, then a ㇆ going right and turning down and a ノ going down left of it, both hanging from the level stroke. 13 characters of the Taiwan set, 88 of the big set: every one Make Me a Hanzi lists with a 方, and 贅, 聱 and 鏊, whose 敖 has a 方 there; `tests/shortcuts.test.js` checks no other character is found. Where it is the only second order it is marked `altOrderIsFang`, and a review notes `'other'` for it. Where there is one already (愣's 忄 in traditional), the 方 is swapped in it too: the two part in separate places, so each may be written either way, and the order noted is the 忄's. Display, hints and the animation keep the data's order. The writing grid writes 方 and 旁.
- **Limits:** characters whose shapes differ between the sets (為) accept only the MOE order. Traditional characters outside the Taiwan set come from the big set and so follow the mainland order, with no MOE alternative, except for 艹 and 忄 (above, and "The big set's 艹 in 丨 一 丨 一").

## Strokes in a second shape

- **The mainland shape of a stroke is accepted too** (the owner, 2026-10-30, used to it: "why not allow both?"), as the mainland order is, with no penalty and no note. The Taiwan set writes some strokes in another shape in the same place: the stroke under 人 in 食 (and 飯, 餓, 館…) is nearly flat, the mainland one a dot; the top of 戶 in 房 is a ノ, not a dot; the last stroke of the 𠂉 in 藍; dots of 雨 (雪, 電, 零…). Display, hints, the animation and the printed character stay Taiwan's.
- **Found when the stroke data is prepared** (`findAlternativeShapes` in `scripts/strokeOrder.js`, from `scripts/copy-char-data.js`; `TAIWAN_SET_VERSION` 6): for a character in both sets, a big-set stroke whose middle point is within 80 units of a Taiwan stroke's (each the other's nearest), that `fitsStroke` would refuse for it, running at least 20° in another direction (`OTHER_SHAPE_MIN_TURN`; nearer, it is the same stroke with some detail, like the short stroke of 藍's 臣), and not all but another stroke. If the two sets have different numbers of strokes, a stroke is paired only with the big set's stroke of the same number, so only the strokes before the part that differs count (the owner, 2026-11-05: the dot on top of 戶 refused in 遍, whose 辶 has a stroke more in Taiwan's set; this adds 遍, 腿's flat stroke inside 月, and a stroke of 還's 衣). Stored as `altShapes` (`{ strokeNum: median }`): in 85 of the 1,013 characters. 腿's flat stroke is refused at half size, top left, like 髒's (so 腿 is not in the writing grid).
- **The inside of 囪 like a 夕, in 聰 and 總** (the owner, 2026-11-12, in 聰). Taiwan's form crosses ノ ノ 丶 like a #; the big set writes the second ノ as a ㇇. The search above misses it twice (the big set's 丶 crosses the Taiwan ノ's middle, so the two aren't paired, and the ㇇ points only 15° to 19° away), so for the characters in `WINDOW_CHARS` (`scripts/strokeOrder.js`) strokes are paired by number with no turn asked for (`windowShapes`): stroke 11 of each, nothing else. Other characters with 囪 (窗, 蔥, 匆) aren't in the Taiwan set and already come with the ㇇. The writing grid writes 聰 and 總 so.
- **While writing** (`otherShapeFor` in `src/quiz.js`): a stroke Hanzi Writer refuses is accepted if it fits the expected stroke's second shape (moved into the frame, leniency times the size as in `isExpectedStrokeAtAnotherSize`) and lies nearer to it than to any stroke after it; one of those within twice the distance (`NEAR_IN_PLACE`) must also be less like it in shape, or a stroke written too early passes for it (5 such in the measurement before this rule, 0 after). **A later stroke of the same shape (a twin) doesn't count: the order decides** (`TWIN_SHAPE_DIFFERENCE`, 0.05 of `shapeDifferenceFromStroke`; twins measure below 0.035, other strokes above 0.09). The owner writes the 王 of 聽 lower under 耳 than the data has it, so the level stroke on top lay as near the second as the first, and was refused up to eight times in their recording (2026-10-31); no share of the two distances told their strokes from a twin written too early. So the first of two such strokes written is the first of them, wherever between the two it lies: a twin written too early passes (房's second stroke for the dot on top; also in 害, 屬, 望, 鐵, 雪, 雲, 零, 電), and the stroke after it is refused, which shows what was skipped. A first stroke written anywhere may be in the second shape too (`firstStrokeAnywhere`). The accepted stroke is noted against the second shape, so the frame fits it.
- **Measured** (`tests/strokeOrder.test.js`): the second shapes shaken, shifted and resized as for the stroke orders, 670 of 686 accepted, and no later stroke of the character taken for one, except twins. The writing grid writes 食, 房, 藍, 雨, 聽 and 遍 with the mainland shapes at every position.
- **A dot may be written steeply down** (the owner, 2026-11-04: the dot under 人 in 飽 refused, written as a downward dot rather than a line). The mainland shapes of the short dots going down-right (the dot under 人 in 食 and 飠, the dots of 雨, 今, 冷…) run only 20° to 50° below level, and a stroke about 20° off is refused, so each also has two steep shapes: the same length around the same middle, turned to 60° and 85° below level (`steepDotsOf` in `src/strokeMatch.js`, `STEEP_DOT_DIRECTIONS`), tried in `otherShapeFor` after the stored one with the same rules. Together they cover from level to straight down. Measured (`tests/strokeOrder.test.js`): 539 of 546 shaken steep dots accepted, no later stroke taken for one. The writing grid writes 食 and 飽 with the dot straight down.
- **Limits:** only characters in the Taiwan set (戶 written alone isn't, so it shows and asks for the big set's form, with a dot); simplified writing doesn't accept the Taiwan shapes; 艹 in 3 strokes is not this (a join and a reorder, see "Out of scope so far" above).

## Strokes of the same shape

- **A stroke lying nearer a later stroke of the same shape is accepted: the order decides** (the owner, 2026-11-06, from their recordings of 經 and 發: 巛's first stroke refused up to fourteen times, 癶's first ノ on the right up to five). Hanzi Writer refuses a stroke that also fits a later stroke lying nearer, by judging it with 0.3 to 0.6 of the leniency. The owner writes 經's 巛 and the right half of 發's 癶 further right than the frame (fitted mostly on the left part) puts them, so each first stroke lay on the second. Almost every refusal passed all of Hanzi Writer's checks at 1.8 and failed only after that cut.
- **Twins** (`areTwins` in `src/strokeMatch.js`): two textbook strokes whose shapes differ by under 0.09 (`TWIN_SHAPE_LIMIT`), each compared as printed and without its first point (發's two ノ both begin with a short dab; without it they differ by 0.069), and whose lengths are within 1.7 times (`TWIN_LENGTH_RATIO`; so 上's short and long level strokes are no twins). In the Taiwan set 7,099 of 59,630 pairs of strokes in a character are twins.
- **While writing** (`twinStrokeFor` in `src/quiz.js`, verdict `twin` in a raw input recording): a refused stroke is accepted if it fits the expected stroke (`fitsStroke`, in the frame, leniency times the size as for second shapes), a later twin lies less than twice as far, and every later stroke that is no twin lies further off and, within twice the distance, is less like it in shape (as `otherShapeFor`). So a twin written too early passes, and the stroke after it is refused, which shows what was skipped.
- **Measured** (`tests/strokeOrder.test.js`, the Taiwan set): no later stroke of another shape is ever taken for the expected one (any of the shaken variations); a stroke moved 60% of the way toward a twin close by is accepted 1,730 times of 2,204, the rest lying as near a stroke of another shape (交's dot toward its level stroke passes the ノ between). The owner's six recorded writings, replayed in the app: each first try accepted and the character finished without a mistake; before, 6 to 10 mistakes each (`local/strictness/replay.browser.js`, git-ignored).
- **Not built: 氣's dots in 米** (the owner, same recordings): written lower and further left than the frame puts them, next to 米's level stroke, which is no twin. Moving the reference by how far off the last stroke was would pass 2 of their 3 first tries, but changes how every character is followed; not done, and not waited on (the owner, 2026-11-12: the characters once waiting on recordings mostly feel better). 樂 has passed since the leniency became 1.8.

## Gestures on the writing area

A must-have on mobile for the owner (2026-10-15), who chose two; a double tap for a hint was dropped as "bad memory". Tone cards have no gestures (the owner, that day), since a stroke there is the answer. Whether a touch was a gesture is decided in `src/gestures.js`; `src/quiz.js` acts on it.

- **How they are caught before Hanzi Writer grades them.** Hanzi Writer takes every touch that moves for a stroke. The browser reports a touch as pointer events before the touch or mouse events Hanzi Writer listens to, so `quiz.js` follows each touch with `pointerdown`/`pointermove`/`pointerup`. On a gesture it calls the quiz off (`writer.cancelQuiz()`), which also throws away the line being drawn. Gestures are taken only while a stroke can be written (`writing`, and not while busy).
- **A long hold shows the next stroke as a hint.** The finger or pen stays within `HOLD_STILLNESS_PX` (12) of where it landed for `HOLD_MS` (0.4 s; 0.5 until the owner, 2026-10-24, found it a bit slow). A dot (點) moves further and is over sooner. The quiz restarts at the same stroke and that stroke flashes green, the same flash as after three misses, drawn and faded in about a second (`HINT_SPEED` in `src/quiz.js`, Hanzi Writer's `strokeHighlightSpeed`; its default was over in 0.4 s, too fast, the owner said the same day). What is drawn after the hold, before lifting, is not graded. A hold is decided only on a timer that came on time and a screen frame later (`holdTimeUp`): a late timer means the page was busy, as while unpacking the offline data, and the movements of a stroke may still be waiting, which first made strokes in the browser tests count as holds. The quiz is busy (`setBusy`) while it restarts after a hold. The writing area carries `data-hint-shown` while a hint has been shown, for the browser tests.
- **How a hint counts is a setting** (`hintCountsAs`, under Writing; the owner, 2026-10-15): "Missed" (`missed`, the default), or "Passed, the word Hard at best" (`hard`). It is read by `judgeChar` and `suggestGrade` in `src/session.js`, and applies to the automatic hint after three misses too. As before, a verdict can be overruled by tapping the character's box.
- **A swipe up wipes the character and starts it again**, exactly as Clear does (`onWipe` → `startOver` in `src/wordFlow.js`). Mistakes made before it still count, except in practice (the Learn tab and a lesson's tracing steps, `freshRestart`; see `docs/cards.md`). **Once a character is written it works too** (2026-10-17): in Review it starts practicing the character again, which changes nothing ("Practicing a written word again" in `docs/cards.md`); elsewhere it starts it again as Clear does. A hold does nothing then. A swipe rises by at least a third of the writing area (the owner, 2026-10-20: half was too tall, the owner's swipes rising 36 to 40%), leans at most about 22° from vertical, does not first go down by more than a tenth of its rise, and **is quick: at most 300 ms from the finger landing to its lifting** (`isSwipeUp`, `SWIPE_MAX_MS`; the owner, 2026-10-20, after tester 3's slow line upward wiped the character; the owner's swipes take 120 to 150 ms). **A readout for testers**, "Show how each swipe up measured" under Settings › For testing (`swipeReadout`, a device setting; `showSwipeReadout` in `src/quiz.js`, `describeSwipe` in `src/gestures.js`), shows each upward touch's rise, lean, dip and time against what a swipe needs, at the top of the screen for 6 seconds. Off by default. A slow line upward is a stroke, graded as any other. The tip, Clear's `title` and the setting's hint say "swipe up quickly".
- **Stricter before a steeply rising stroke** (the owner, 2026-10-15: in Skritter, writing the 提 of 氵 in 滿 big can wipe the character). The 提 of 氵 can be nearly as upright as a swipe (淹's leans 14°) and is up to 0.4 of the character's height, so steepness cannot tell them apart; only length can. When the stroke expected next ends higher than it starts and rises more than it goes sideways (`risesSteeply`), a swipe must rise half the writing area (three quarters until the owner, 2026-10-20, found it failed their own swipes; half is still above the tallest such stroke written a fifth bigger than the stroke data's box, 0.48). Only the stroke Hanzi Writer expects next is looked at, not the other stroke order's.
- **Tested against every stroke of both stroke data sets** (`tests/gestures.test.js`): only steeply rising strokes ever have a swipe's shape (about 470 of 123,178), so the stricter rule guards them whenever they come next; at the size the app draws them, 3 reach a third of the area (a risk only when written out of order); no steeply rising one is a strict swipe at 1.2 times the stroke data's box. (The app draws the stroke data box at 0.88 of the area, a margin of 6% each side.) `tests/browser/gestures.browser.js` holds and swipes in the app, on 十 and before the 提 of 滿.
- **Found only through a tip** (`gestures` in `src/tips.js`, below Clear, the first time a character can be written), Clear's `title`, and the hint under the setting.
