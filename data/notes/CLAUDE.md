# Writing learning notes

Read before writing or checking notes in this folder. Moved word for word from the "Where the work stands" section of CLAUDE.md on 2026-10-15.

- **Wiktionary's data is kept locally for development** (owner, 2026-10-13: no asking kaikki.org's server character by character): `local/` is git-ignored except its README, which says how to fetch the 155 MB file. `scripts/wiktionary-glyph-origins.js` makes a small file of glyph origins from it, and `scripts/character-facts.js <list file> <level>` prints, for a level's characters, their parts (Make Me a Hanzi) and glyph origin (Wiktionary) as reference material for writing notes. None of it is shipped. Each computer needs its own copy.
- **A second run of the notes pilot** was started with that material and a tighter brief (parts of characters from the sources only, with meaning part and sound part; no needless "do not confuse"; no filler). The first run is `data/notes/hsk-3.0-1.tsv`. Notes that lean on Wiktionary are to be CC BY-SA 4.0.
- **Decided by the owner on the notes (2026-10-13), after reading the second run:** go on with them at that quality and cost. A note never names its source ("Wiktionary says…"); the credit goes in the data's license notice. Every word has an example sentence, always stored in both scripts and shown in both only when both scripts are being learned. Parts of characters stay in a note only where they matter for sound or meaning and the word page's clickable parts cannot say it (the parts are to be tagged "meaning" and "sound" there, from Make Me a Hanzi). **Wanted on the word page:** a button to add the example sentence to a deck, in the script or scripts being learned (needs sentences as items, step 3).
- **The notes for HSK 3.0 level 1 are in `data/notes/hsk-3.0-1.tsv`** (the second run, with the 160 notes that named Wiktionary reworded by a further Sonnet pass), CC BY-SA 4.0; the folder's README says how they were made and that no fluent speaker has checked them. `src/notes.js` reads them and splits an example sentence into its words by the spaces in its pinyin (`sentenceWords`); `src/characters.js` reads Make Me a Hanzi's dictionary and gives a character's parts with their roles (`structureOf`).
- **Which script a note's own text uses** (the owner, 2026-10-13): simplified is fine for the notes of the HSK lists; the notes written for the TOCFL lists are to use traditional characters. (The example sentence is in both either way.) So a traditional learner reading an HSK word's note sees simplified characters inside the English text; that is accepted.
- **Notes for HSK 3.0 levels 2 to 4** (`data/notes/hsk-3.0-2.tsv` to `-4.tsv`; levels 3 and 4 written on 2026-10-14 by Sonnet subagents, two or three per level, to a brief in the session's scratchpad that is not kept). What the checking found, for the next run: writers skip the character material unless told to look up every character; they write the spoken tone changes of 一 and 不 (yì, bú) although asked for yī and bù; they put Taiwan's usual form in a traditional sentence instead of the list's (報導 for 報道), which breaks the link to the word; and a word the list has twice with the same simplified form and reading (了解/瞭解) must get one note, since notes are found by those two.
- **Notes for the two beginner levels of the TOCFL** (`data/notes/tocfl-novice.tsv`, written 2026-10-13): the 74 words of Novice 1 and 2 that had no note from the HSK levels (76 until 2026-10-14, when 多少 and 告诉 were given their right readings and so met their notes in HSK level 1), with the note's Chinese in traditional characters and Taiwan's usage first, as the owner asked.
- **Notes for TOCFL levels 1 to 3** (`data/notes/tocfl-1.tsv` to `tocfl-3.tsv`, 2026-10-14): the 44, 112 and 301 words no other file covers, written the same way. Their run is what found the five TOCFL readings put right that day.
- **Notes for TOCFL level 4** (`data/notes/tocfl-4.tsv`, 2026-10-14): 1,135 words, four Sonnet agents of 284 words each, with the Taiwanese reader's corrections in the brief. Its run found one more reading: 夾, which the committee lists as jiá (Taiwan's reading of "to clip") and the build had matched to jiá "lined, double-layered"; a 27th line in `reading-fixes.tsv`. After the run, 11 spoken tone changes of 一 and 不 were put back, and 17 sentences had their pinyin spaced so that the word stands alone (a particle split off, "wàng zhe"); 59 single characters are still shown inside a compound (大象), as allowed. One agent was stopped by the API's content filter while checking its work, after all its notes were written; nobody could tell what set it off.
- **No bare remarks on the other script's form** (the owner, 2026-10-14, on "The traditional 愛人 is the same word"). A sweep that day took about a hundred such sentences out of the HSK notes ("The traditional form is 寫", "务 is the simplified 務", "the traditional 禮 is more complicated") and corrected five that were wrong (其余, 夜间, 此后, 光线, 两岸). A note mentions the other script only where it explains something: one simplified character standing for two (发 for 發 and 髮), or a part that changed (進 has 隹). Check new notes for this.
- **Notes for a native speaker to check** (2026-10-14): the notes whose writers were unsure, and those making claims about region, register or politeness, are on two private pages for the owner to share (a Taiwan one and a general one, made from a script in that session's scratchpad, not kept). Their answers are to be worked in when they come. **The Taiwan page was answered on 2026-10-14** by a Taiwanese reader (through the owner; silence on a note meant it was fine) and worked in the same day: 23 notes corrected, and 5 more that repeated the same claims. What it taught, for future notes about Taiwan: people say 中文 (國語 is the official name and sounds formal or old); 機車 is a scooter, 摩托車 an ordinary motorcycle, 重機 a big one; Taiwan says 網路, never 網絡, and writes only 著; a city bus is 公車; 姊/姐 and 念/唸 are both fine; 鼻水 and 鼻涕 are two things, both used; drinks are ordered 正常冰, 少冰, 微冰, 去冰; most people say 熊貓; 捷運 even for Japan's metro; never write 台灣地區 (a mainland framing); and remarks like "the word is the same in Taiwan" are filler. **The general page (131 notes) is being answered by a mainland reader**, through the owner, in batches. **Notes #1 to #20 were answered on 2026-10-15** and worked in the same day (silence or "yes" meant fine): 对不起 is not literally "cannot face you" but "not do right by you", the negative of 对得起, and is not used for sympathy; the 气 of 生气 stands for 怒气 "anger", not "breath"; 饭店 is a restaurant on the mainland and a hotel in Taiwan (and in some names, 北京饭店), so 高级饭店 in the note of 高级 became 高级酒店 and 宾馆's note was put right; 爱人 is a spouse, a rather formal word, never "lover", and 情人 in everyday mainland speech usually means a partner in an affair (added to the TOCFL note too); a 酒店 is more upscale than a 宾馆 or 旅馆. Usage points they added went into 常常, 就, 越, 一直, 又, 才, 快要 and 就要, cut to one short sentence or example each. **A reader's answer is condensed, not copied in whole** (the owner, 2026-10-15, wary of notes turning into blocks of text): a typical HSK note is about 125 characters and 9 in 10 are under 180; a correction may make one longer, but extras are trimmed to the point a learner needs. The reader's answers stay on the owner's devices, outside the repository. **Notes #21 to #42 were answered on 2026-10-17** (the reader's batch was #1 to #40; #41 and #42 were covered by their answers to #40 and #29) and worked into 15 notes the same day: 搬's 扌 is called 提手旁; 救过我一次 is more usual than 救了; 寸 is cùn, and 小村 is literary (小村子 in speech, so the sentence changed); 并不 denies a state, habit or wish and 并没有 an event, and 并 also joins verbs in writing (请阅读课文并回答问题); 就是 got an example for each of its three uses; 只是 against 就是 (我只是想帮帮你 / 我就是想帮你); 才能 is a noun, and 才+能 the adverb; 年前 is short for 过年前 ("northern" taken out, not confirmed); 散文家; 弹琴 is plucked or keyed; 一声 is yìshēng as a measure and yīshēng as the first tone; 依旧 is often nostalgic; the 总裁 runs the business and the 董事长 heads the 董事会; 协议 stresses agreement, 合同 legal rights and duties; a 评论 can be neutral, 争议 means disagreement. **Notes #43 to #61 were answered on 2026-10-21** (their "#60" was the page's #61, 生活; #60 出生 got no answer, so it is fine) and worked into 10 notes the same day: 网友 is anyone you know through the internet, even after meeting in person, and also anyone posting online; 过's uses numbered with their part of speech; 京 as in 京城, and Beijing was 北平 from 1928 to 1949; 南边 is the south side of something, not necessarily nearby; 来自 is formal (in speech 我是北京人/北京的); 离 got an example of distance; 通过 an example for each use; 后来 is only about the past, but 以后 is past or future (the note had said 以后 was for the future); 年轻 is for teenagers and adults, and a child is 小; 住 is only about where you live, 生活 all of daily life. - **The Chinese in a note can be tapped** for its reading and meaning (since 2026-10-15; `docs/word-page.md`), so notes need no pinyin written in. The app splits it into words by the dictionary, taking the longest word it knows at each point, so a phrase in a note should be made of ordinary dictionary words.
- **No word gets a note twice** (the owner, 2026-10-13). Notes are found by a word's simplified characters and pinyin, whichever list it came from, so before notes are written for another level or list (HSK 2.0, TOCFL), the words that already have a note in any file of `data/notes/` are taken out of the input.
- **Notes exist for the radicals** (`data/notes/radicals.tsv`: 60 written 2026-10-13, the other 96 of the deck of radicals 2026-10-14) and are shown on the page of a part of a character, and on a radical's own page.

A Sonnet subagent was writing them for HSK 3.0 level 1 (509 words) as a trial of quality; it came back with 509 notes for about 198,000 tokens.
