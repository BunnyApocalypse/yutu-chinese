            Characters

            Chinese is written in characters, not letters. Each character is one syllable, and most have a meaning of their own: 人 "person", 大 "big", 山 "mountain".

            A word is one character or a few: 火 "fire" and 山 "mountain" make 火山 "volcano".

            Many characters have two forms: simplified, used in mainland China, and traditional, used in Taiwan and Hong Kong, like 学 and 學 "study". You learn the one you chose in Settings, or both.

            Pinyin

            Pinyin spells the sounds of Chinese in the Latin alphabet: 火山 is huǒshān. The app shows it with the characters, and it is how Chinese is typed.

            The marks over the vowels, as in huǒshān, are the tones: more on them next.

            Most letters sound roughly as in English. These don't:

            • x as "sh" in "sheep", with a smile: xī
            • q as "ch" in "cheap": qī
            • c as "ts" in "cats": cī
            • z as "ds" in "kids": zì
            • zh as "j" in "jam", the tongue curled back: zhī
            • ü as "ee" said with rounded lips: lǜ

            After z, c, s, zh, ch, sh and r, the i is not "ee" but a buzz that carries the sound on, as in zì and zhī.

            Tones

            Every syllable has a tone: its pitch stays high, rises, dips or falls. Another tone makes another word, so the tone is part of learning each word.

            • mā "mother": first tone, high and level
            • má "hemp": second tone, rising, as in "What?"
            • mǎ "horse": third tone, low, dipping
            • mà "to scold": fourth tone, falling, as in a firm "No!"
            • māma "mom": the second ma is neutral, short and light, with no mark

            A few tones change beside others: two third tones in a row are said as a second and a third, so nǐ hǎo sounds like ní hǎo. Pinyin writes the tone each syllable has on its own.

            Writing

            Characters are made of strokes, written in a set order. As a rule: top before bottom, left before right, and a horizontal stroke before a vertical one crossing it, as in 十.

            The app checks each stroke as you write. Stuck? Hold your finger or pen still on the writing area for a hint. Gone wrong? Swipe up quickly to start the character again.

            You don't have to be neat: your writing stays as you drew it, and the usual shortcuts of handwriting are accepted.

            Four kinds of card

            Each word you learn becomes up to four cards, and each comes back just before you would forget it:

            Meaning
            See the word, remember what it means.
            Pinyin
            See the word, remember how it is said.
            Tone
            Give each character's tone: tap a tone button, or draw the mark on the card. A dot is the neutral tone.
            Writing
            See the meaning, write the word.

            New words come in Learn, with a short lesson each. Review brings the cards back; after each one, say how it went.

            Cards

              Start over makes a card new again. Suspend keeps a card from coming up, with its history and schedule, until resumed. I know this word answers its new cards "Easy", so the word skips its lesson and the cards come up in Review in some days.

                What's the difference?

                Simplified characters are used in mainland China, Singapore and Malaysia. Traditional characters are used in Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau. Many characters are the same in both; others differ, like 学 and 學.

                With both, your cards are in the script you mostly use, and you can pick the other one for single words.

                Pronunciation
                Pronunciation: which standard?

                A few words are read differently in Taiwan and on the mainland: 星期 is xīngqí in Taiwan and xīngqī on the mainland (more on Taiwan's Mandarin). The reading you choose is the one shown and tested. With Taiwan, the mainland reading also appears beside it in small print, because dictionaries file words under it. With Accept both, cards show both readings and accept either tone.
