These are the word lists of the two official tests of Chinese for learners, the HSK and the TOCFL. They are a common answer to "which words should I learn first?", and many textbooks and courses follow them. The lowest level is the easiest. Not sure? Start with HSK 3.0, level 1. The radicals, at the end, go well alongside any of them.

            Cards

              Start over makes a card new again. Suspend keeps a card from coming up, with its history and schedule, until resumed. I know this word answers its new cards "Easy", so the word skips its lesson and the cards come up in Review in some days.

              Import backup

              Combine keeps what is on this device and adds what the backup has: for studying on two devices. A backup of this device is saved to your downloads first, in case you want to go back.
              Replace throws away what is on this device and puts the backup in its place.

              Import from Anki

              Bring your words over from Anki: a deck exported as an .apkg file, or your whole collection (.colpkg). Their schedules come along, so cards are due when Anki said.

              In Anki: File › Export, then "Anki Deck Package" with "Include scheduling information" ticked. Audio and pictures are not brought over.

              Decks to bring

              Cards with no history in Anki (usually writing) come a few a day in Review, with a lesson, as many as your new words per day.

                What's the difference?

                Simplified characters are used in mainland China, Singapore and Malaysia. Traditional characters are used in Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau. Many characters are the same in both; others differ, like 学 and 學.

                With both, your cards are in the script you mostly use, and you can pick the other one for single words.

                Pronunciation
                Pronunciation: which standard?

                A few words are read differently in Taiwan and on the mainland: 星期 is xīngqí in Taiwan and xīngqī on the mainland (more on Taiwan's Mandarin). The reading you choose is the one shown and tested. With Taiwan, the mainland reading also appears beside it in small print, because dictionaries file words under it. With Accept both, cards show both readings and accept either tone.

                Card types
                Sentences Sentences are added from a word's page (its example sentence) or on a deck's page. A sentence added without a word to fill in is written whole.

                How much of a dictionary entry is filled in as the meaning when you add a word. You can still edit it before saving.
                First only: just the first meaning.
                Everyday: every meaning except those marked for a specialist field, such as "(math.)" or "(law)".
                All: everything the dictionary lists.

                Daily study

                When a word you already know is to be written for the first time (after changing script, say): its tones, then each stroke shown, then the outline, then from memory. Words in the Learn tab are always taught this way.

                Writing

                How many strokes the character has, as in "3/7". It is always shown in Learn and Free practice; in Review it is a hint, so it is off unless you want it.

                Free practice: write any character or word, or try any kind of card. Nothing is saved, and your schedule is not touched.
