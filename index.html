These are the word lists of the two official tests of Chinese for learners, the HSK and the TOCFL. They are a common answer to "which words should I learn first?", and many textbooks and courses follow them. The lowest level is the easiest. Not sure? Start with HSK 3.0, level 1. The radicals, at the end, go well alongside any of them.

              Welcome to Hanzi Trainer

              This app helps you learn to read and write Chinese words. It brings each word back just before you would forget it.

              Everything stays on this device. There is no account, and nothing is sent anywhere.

              The next few screens set the app up and show how it works. It takes about a minute.

              Look

              Which characters are you learning?

              Script

              Simplified characters are used in mainland China, Singapore and Malaysia. Traditional characters are used in Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau. Many characters are the same in both; others differ, like 学 and 學. If you are not sure, pick the one used where you plan to read or speak Chinese.

              With both, your cards are in the script you mostly use, and you can pick the other one for single words.

              Pronunciation: which standard?

              A few words are read differently in Taiwan and on the mainland: 星期 is xīngqí in Taiwan and xīngqī on the mainland (more on Taiwan's Mandarin). The reading you choose is the one shown and tested. With Taiwan, the mainland reading also appears beside it in small print, because dictionaries file words under it. With Both, cards show both readings and accept either tone.

              You can change all of this later in Settings.

              Four kinds of card

              Each word is practiced in up to four ways. Each kind has its own schedule, so you may know what a word means long before you can write it.

              Cards you want

              Untick the kinds you don't want. A new word starts with its first kind in this order and gets the others one by one.

              Your words

              The app starts with no words. To begin with, it can add the first level of one of the two official tests' word lists; the words then wait in Learn, a few a day.

              Start with

              Everything is in the menu, ☰ at the top left; each screen explains itself with a short tip the first time. More levels, the radicals, and lists or words of your own can be added any time in Decks. Tap any word there to see its page: its learning tips, an example sentence, and the parts its characters are built from. If something about a word is wrong, Edit on that page corrects it; after you answer one of its cards, About and Edit do the same.

              Keeping your data

              Your words and your review history are stored in this browser, on this device, and nowhere else.

              A browser may delete what a site has stored, without asking, when the device runs short of space. To rule that out, the app needs the browser's permission to store its data for the long term.

              Either way, Export backup in Settings saves everything as one file. Do that now and then; it is also how you move to another device.

              The app also saves itself and its stroke data on this device (about 18 MB to download), so it works without a connection. On a phone or tablet, install it from the browser's menu: "Install app" or "Add to Home Screen".

              Settings

              Chinese

              Simplified characters are used in mainland China, Singapore and Malaysia. Traditional characters are used in Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau. Many characters are the same in both; others differ, like 学 and 學. If you are not sure, pick the one used where you plan to read or speak Chinese.
              With one script, you are only asked for and shown that one. With both, cards are in the script named, and single words can be given the other.

              A few words are read differently in Taiwan and on the mainland: 星期 is xīngqí in Taiwan and xīngqī on the mainland (more on Taiwan's Mandarin). The reading you choose is the one shown and tested. With Taiwan, the mainland reading also appears beside it in small print, because dictionaries file words under it. With Both, cards show both readings and accept either tone. With Mainland China, Taiwan readings are never shown or asked for.

              Card types
              Sentences Sentences are added from a word's page (its example sentence) or on a deck's page. A sentence added without a word to fill in is written whole.

              How much of a dictionary entry is filled in as the meaning when you add a word. You can still edit it before saving.
              First only: just the first meaning.
              Everyday: every meaning except those marked for a specialist field, such as "(math.)" or "(law)".
              All: everything the dictionary lists.

              Daily study

              When a word you already know is to be written for the first time (after changing script, say): its tones, then each stroke shown, then the outline, then from memory. Words in the Learn tab are always taught this way.

              Writing

              How many strokes the character has, as in "3/7". It is always shown in Learn and Free practice; in Review it is a hint, so it is off unless you want it.
