These are the word lists of the two official tests of Chinese for learners, the HSK and the TOCFL. They are a common answer to "which words should I learn first?", and many textbooks and courses follow them. The lowest level is the easiest. Not sure? Start with HSK 3.0, level 1.

Levels

What you add appears under My decks, a deck for each level. Where a level is divided into smaller groups of words, each group is a deck inside it.

A deck is a group of words: a textbook, say, with a deck inside it for each chapter. Tap a deck to see its words and add to them. A word can be in several decks and is still scheduled once.

          Welcome to Hanzi Trainer

          This app helps you learn to read and write Chinese words. It brings each word back just before you would forget it.

          Everything stays on this device. There is no account, and nothing is sent anywhere.

          The next few screens set the app up and show how it works. It takes about a minute.

          Which characters are you learning?

          Script

          Simplified characters are used in mainland China, Singapore and Malaysia. Traditional characters are used in Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau. Many characters are the same in both; others differ, like 学 and 學. If you are not sure, pick the one used where you plan to read or speak Chinese.

          With both, your cards are in the script you mostly use, and you can pick the other one for single words.

          Pronunciation

          Some words are read differently in Taiwan: 星期 is xīngqī on the mainland and xīngqí in Taiwan (more on Taiwan's Mandarin). With both, a card shows both readings and accepts both tones.

          You can change all of this later in Settings.

          Four kinds of card

          Each word is practiced in up to four ways. Each kind has its own schedule, so you may know what a word means long before you can write it.

          Cards you want

          Untick the kinds you don't want. A new word starts with its first kind in this order and gets the others one by one.

          How it works

          Learn teaches you the words that are waiting, one at a time: you look at the word, practice its tones, write it stroke by stroke as each stroke is shown, and then write it from memory. A few a day is plenty (the goal is 5 to begin with), so that reviews don't pile up.

          Review brings back the words you have learned. It shows the card that is due next; after you answer, you grade yourself: Again, Hard, Good or Easy. Each button says when the card would come back, and where the app can check your answer it suggests a grade.

          Writing: write anywhere in the box, at any size. Your own strokes stay on screen, and two strokes joined the way handwriting joins them are accepted.

          Free practice lets you try any card, or write any characters at all. Nothing there is recorded.

          Your words

          The app starts with no words. You add the ones you want to learn in the Decks tab; they then wait in the Learn tab until you learn them.

          Type a word's characters and tap its dictionary entry to fill in the reading and meaning, paste a list of your own, or add a ready-made list such as the words of HSK level 1.

          If something about a word is wrong, correct it in the Decks tab, or tap Edit word after answering one of its cards.

          Keeping your data

          Your words and your review history are stored in this browser, on this device, and nowhere else.

          A browser may delete what a site has stored, without asking, when the device runs short of space. To rule that out, the app needs the browser's permission to store its data for the long term.

          Either way, Export backup in Settings saves everything as one file. Do that now and then; it is also how you move to another device.

          The app also saves itself and its stroke data on this device (about 18 MB to download), so it works without a connection. On a phone or tablet, install it from the browser's menu: "Install app" or "Add to Home Screen".
